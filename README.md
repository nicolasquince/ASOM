Frequently Asked Questions

What is the main objective of the ASOM project?

The primary objective of ASOM is to investigate the feasibility of a low-cost mission architecture for the rapid in-situ exploration of Near-Earth Asteroids. The project focuses on enabling multiple sequential asteroid flybys using a small constellation of identical small simple spacecraft, maximizing scientific return while minimizing mission complexity and cost.

What makes ASOM different from traditional asteroid missions?

Unlike conventional missions that target a single asteroid over many years, ASOM is designed around a multi-target reconnaissance approach. By leveraging solar-electric propulsion and optimized trajectory planning in near-translunar space, a single spacecraft can encounter many asteroids within one mission lifetime. With a three-spacecraft constellation 100+ asteroids can be visited, enabling broader statistical characterization of the NEA population.

Is ASOM a real mission or a conceptual study?

ASOM is a fully developed research mission concept validated through high-fidelity simulations and system-level design. While it is not currently scheduled for launch, it demonstrates technical feasibility using existing or near-term technologies and serves as a realistic reference architecture for future small-body exploration missions.
